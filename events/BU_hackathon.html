Project Overview

During the Boston University High School Hackathon, our team tackled traffic congestion modeling using graph theory and mathematical simulation. We specifically investigated Braess's Paradox—the counterintuitive phenomenon where adding one or more roads to a road network can actually increase overall travel times for all drivers, and conversely, closing certain congested shortcuts can reduce collective delay.

Technical Implementation

  • OpenStreetMap & OSMnx: Downloaded and constructed real topological road network graphs of Boston, Massachusetts.
  • Bureau of Public Roads (BPR) Congestion Modeling: Computed non-linear latency curves based on road speed limits, lane capacity, and vehicle flow volume.
  • Greedy Road Deletion Algorithm: Simulated driver equilibrium before and after road removal to identify network bottlenecks that, when shut down, optimize city-wide throughput.
