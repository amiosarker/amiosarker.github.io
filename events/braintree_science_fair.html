🌟 Event Overview

The Braintree Science Fair was organized as an inspiring district-wide demonstration of everything that East Middle School and Braintree High School have to offer in the fields of science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM). The fair brought together students, teachers, families, and community members to experience hands-on experiments, scientific inquiry, and student-built engineering projects across grade levels.

🤖 Representing the Robotics Club

At the event, I represented the Robotics Club to give visitors a firsthand look at how software and physical hardware merge in robotics. The centerpiece of our demonstration was my MediaPipe Hand Tracking & Servo Control robotic hand (featured on the Projects Page).

Our booth served as an engaging live demonstration:

  • Interactive Public Demos: Visitors and younger students stood in front of the camera, moved their own hands, and watched in real time as the mechanical hand mirrored their finger gestures and grip positions.
  • Connecting Software and Hardware: Attendees were shown how modern computer vision pipelines (Google MediaPipe) detect 3D joint landmarks from standard camera frames and translate those mathematical coordinates into physical motor commands.
  • Highlighting East Middle & High School Pathways: Demonstrating this project highlighted the exciting STEM and robotics pathways available to students advancing from East Middle School into Braintree High School.

⚙️ How the Demonstration Works

The demonstration combines real-time computer vision in Python with low-level micro-controller hardware:

  • Camera & MediaPipe Tracking: OpenCV captures high-framerate webcam frames, while Google MediaPipe detects 21 hand landmarks in 3D space.
  • Dynamic Joint Angle Math: Python calculates relative vector angles between joint segments for each individual finger.
  • Serial Data Transmission: The computed angles are formatted and streamed via USB serial (at 115200 baud) to an Arduino Mega 2560.
  • Servo Articulation: The Arduino reads the data stream and drives five servo motors powered by an external 5V supply, pulling tendons that actuate the fingers of the robotic hand.

💡 Takeaways & Community Impact

Representing the Robotics Club at the Braintree Science Fair was a fantastic experience. It gave me the opportunity to explain real-world engineering challenges—such as selecting the right servo motor ratings, handling serial latency, and tuning joint angle ranges—to both curious peers and younger students interested in joining the robotics team. Seeing attendees' excitement when controlling the robotic hand live proved how engaging interactive science demonstrations can be!
